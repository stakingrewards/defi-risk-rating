## Executive Summary

weETH (`0xCd5fE23C85820F7B72D0926FC9b05b43E359b7ee`) is the non-rebasing, wrapped representation of eETH (`0x35fA164735182de50811E8e2E824cFb9B6118ac2`), ether.fi's liquid restaking token. Both sit over the same LiquidityPool (`0x308861A430be4cce5502d0A12724771Fc6DaF216`): users deposit native ETH, the protocol stakes it to Beacon Chain validators through a non-custodial DKG model, and restakes through EigenLayer. weETH is the form of the token that is actually integrated across DeFi (Aave, Morpho, Pendle) and — critically for this rating — **it is the form that is bridged cross-chain via LayerZero OFT**. Protocol TVL is approximately **$3.14B** (DeFiLlama, 2026-07-14), down materially from ~$5.8B in February 2026.

This is a **re-rating** built on the just-updated eETH V2 assessment (BBB-/810.7) and the adjudicated outcome of the ether.fi team's rating challenge. All settled decisions from that adjudication are carried forward and independently re-verified against fetched evidence: the four resilience upgrades driven by the April–May 2026 redemption wave (ST-C-02, ST-L-05, ST-L-09, ST-M-01 → Low) are confirmed directly against ether.fi's published stress-testing data (542,792 ETH / 19.6% of TVL redeemed across 1,977 requests over 33 days; median 4.9 days to claimable; 100% claimable within 17 days; worst peg deviation −0.58% on 2026-04-20; zero days more than 1% below fair value). The holds are likewise confirmed: signer identities remain undisclosed (S-KM-03), the incident playbook remains untested (S-KM-08), node-operator certification remains partial (ST-IC-03), no bounded max-loss modeling exists (ST-M-04), and ether.fi's own live slashing-risk page still describes slashing insurance as "**arranging... TBD**" (O-FR-01).

**Two material findings from fresh research move this rating below the eETH baseline, and both are scope-driven rather than platform-driven.** First, the July 2026 security upgrade (blacklister role, expanded oracle committee, additional bounds) has **NOT deployed** — the weETH and eETH proxies were last upgraded on 2025-08-07, and ether.fi's docs still list the blacklister as roadmap targeted at July 2026. Undeployed work cannot raise a current score, so ST-M-04 and the S-KM questions hold. Second, and more consequentially: **weETH is a LayerZero OFT cross-chain token, and the eETH rating marked the three cross-chain questions (ST-IC-09/10/11) as N/A on the stated premise that "no bridge verifier setup [is] applicable."** That premise does not hold for weETH. The framework's mandatory applicability checks require these questions to be scored, and the April 2026 KelpDAO incident — in which a **1-of-1 DVN compromise drained $292M** of rsETH from a LayerZero OFT escrow — makes this the single most consequential live attack surface for any bridged LRT. ether.fi has since hardened materially (message libraries pinned, DVN quorum raised to a strict **4-of-4** of Canary/Horizen/Nethermind/LayerZero Labs, LayerZero's own multisig locked out of the weETH config, per-route on-chain rate limits, bridging deprecated on eight low-activity chains as of 2026-06-30). That earns Low (9) on ST-IC-09. But the caps' values are not published (ST-IC-10 = 3), the verifiers' data-source architecture is not documented (ST-IC-11 = 3), and — decisively — **no audit in the public registry is evidenced as covering the live DVN/verifier configuration.** ether.fi's audits page scopes its 30+ reports to smart-contract reviews "commissioned for each major release," the security blog describes audits as covering "the contracts," and the one bridge-adjacent report (Paladin, *OFT Adapter Migration*, 2024.09.30) is a code audit predating the April 2026 move from 2 DVNs to the 4-of-4 quorum — so it cannot have reviewed the current live config. Under the framework's explicit configuration-scope rule, that is the Mid (3) case for S-SC-01: two-plus code audits, deployment configuration not evidenced in audit scope.

**A verification pass on 2026-07-14 re-scored two further questions, both previously mislabelled "Non-Improvable."** **ST-L-02 rises from 3 to 9**: the Low criterion turns on absorbing ~≥10% of TVL with low impact, and ether.fi absorbed 19.6% at a −0.58% worst deviation — the prior Mid reading treated the "near-total exits over stated window" clause as an independent test, when the framework presents it as the inference *drawn from* that absorption threshold, and it was in any case inconsistent with scoring ST-L-05 and ST-L-09 Low on the identical event. **O-G-02 falls from 3 to 1**: the prior score was computed from ETHFI allocation buckets (~55% insider), but the criterion asks what share of voting power the *top 10 voting addresses* hold. Measured against realized voting power on the three most recent Snapshot proposals, the top ten control **99.7% / 97.0% / 97.2%**, and a **single address casts 58.9% / 64.1% / 68.5%** — past the High threshold of >66%, and failing Low's "no obvious single-coalition dominance" test outright. O-G-02 is not a deal breaker, so no cap applies. Both questions are **Improvable**, not structural: this rating now carries **no Non-Improvable constraints**, and the potential ceiling is a full 900.0.

The result is **BB+ (793.6/900)** — 17.1 points below eETH's BBB- (810.7). Most of the gap is the bridge surface that the eETH rating scoped out and that weETH genuinely carries. **One divergence is a platform-level defect requiring correction upstream: O-G-02 is a platform question, and the eETH rating still carries the superseded allocation-bucket score of 3.** Fundamentals remain strong: 30+ public audit reports from 10+ firms in a public registry, a $500k Immunefi bounty, Hypernative monitoring with a pause-only monitoring key, an on-chain 5% APR rebase cap that auto-rejects anomalous oracle reports, a 6-of-10 Upgrade Safe behind a 10-day timelock, an identified team, and a demonstrated mass-redemption track record that now scores Low across all four liquidity and market resilience questions.

## **FINAL RATING: BB+**

*Elevated Grade — for allocators who actively underwrite and monitor risk. ether.fi weETH rests on genuinely strong foundations: 30+ public audit reports from 10+ firms in an open registry, a $500k Immunefi bounty, Hypernative monitoring wired to a pause-only on-chain kill switch, an on-chain 5% APR rebase cap that automatically rejects anomalous oracle reports, a 6-of-10 Upgrade Safe behind a 10-day timelock (both raised in April 2026), an identified team with a $32.3M institutional raise, and — decisively — a demonstrated mass-redemption track record: 542,792 ETH (19.6% of TVL) redeemed across 1,977 requests over 33 days in April–May 2026, median 4.9 days to claimable, 100% claimable within 17 days, worst peg deviation −0.58%, no depositor haircut, and no measurable addition to Ethereum's shared exit queue. That event retires the "untested resilience" ceiling and lifts four questions to Low.*

*The rating sits one notch below the eETH baseline for one reason: weETH is the bridged token. The April 2026 KelpDAO exploit — a 1-of-1 DVN compromise that drained $292M from a LayerZero OFT escrow and cascaded into ~$196M of bad debt at Aave — establishes cross-chain verification as the defining live risk for any bridged LRT. ether.fi's current configuration is best-in-class (a strict 4-of-4 quorum of Canary/Horizen/Nethermind/LayerZero Labs, pinned message libraries, LayerZero's own multisig locked out, per-route on-chain rate limits, and bridging deprecated on eight low-activity chains), and ST-IC-09 scores Low accordingly. But three gaps persist: the rate-limit values are not published, the verifiers' data-source architecture is not documented, and — most consequentially — no report in the public audit registry is evidenced as covering the live DVN configuration (the audits page scopes its reviews to contract code per release; the one bridge-adjacent report predates the current 4-of-4 quorum), leaving the single most dangerous surface in the stack without evidenced independent review. The remaining constraints are unchanged from the eETH adjudication: signer identities withheld so multisig independence cannot be verified; an incident playbook that is published and SEAL-aligned but not evidenced as tested; partial node-operator certification; no verifiable ring-fenced backstop (ether.fi's own page still says slashing insurance is "TBD"); undisclosed treasury composition; and a ~46% TVL decline through H1 2026 that has not recovered.*

## Key Risks
